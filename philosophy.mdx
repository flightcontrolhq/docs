---
title: "Product philosophy"
description: "Ravion gives developers and agents self-service while keeping platform and DevOps teams in control."
---

Ravion is built around a simple belief: developers and agents should move fast, and platform and DevOps engineers should still control how cloud infrastructure is provisioned, deployed, and operated.

Most teams want to reduce DevOps overhead without giving up safety. PaaS tools are fast until you need deeper control. Raw IaC gives control, but it asks every developer to understand platform internals. A patchwork of deployment, observability, and infrastructure tools slows everyone down.

Ravion is the middle path: developer cloud self-service backed by Terraform, reusable modules, controlled pipelines, and shared visibility.

## How Ravion is designed

Ravion is designed around two primitives: modules and pipelines.

Modules turn infrastructure and application patterns into reusable interfaces. They let platform teams encode the right defaults, permissions, providers, resources, and operational expectations once. Developers and agents can then provision and deploy without writing cloud-specific glue for every project.

Pipelines define how work moves through Ravion. They make provisioning, building, deploying, approvals, policy checks, logs, metrics, and lifecycle events visible in one workflow.

Terraform stays at the center. Ravion does not hide infrastructure behind an opaque abstraction. It gives teams a controlled interface over IaC so they can keep version tracking, resource visibility, drift detection, and audit history.

## Who it's for

Ravion is for platform and DevOps teams that want to offer self-service without becoming a ticket queue.

It is for developers who want to provision, build, and deploy without learning every detail of ECS, EKS, Lambda, queues, databases, load balancers, IAM, DNS, and certificates.

It is for teams adopting agents that need safe interfaces to cloud workflows. Agents should be able to act, but only inside the controls your platform team defines.

It is for organizations that want one place to understand infrastructure, deployments, logs, metrics, costs, and changes over time.

## Who it's not for

Ravion is not a replacement for engineering judgment or platform ownership.

It is not the right fit if you want a completely opaque PaaS that hides infrastructure decisions from your team. Ravion keeps the underlying cloud and Terraform model visible.

It is also not the right fit if every team needs to handcraft every resource from scratch with no shared patterns, approvals, or guardrails. Ravion works best when you want reusable modules and repeatable workflows.

## Principles

- Self-service should come with guardrails, not gatekeeping.
- Developers and agents should use clear interfaces instead of rewriting platform logic.
- DevOps should keep control over standards, permissions, approvals, and lifecycle behavior.
- Infrastructure should stay inspectable, versioned, and auditable.
- Workflows should connect provisioning, builds, deploys, logs, metrics, and cost.
- Teams should avoid lock-in by building on Terraform and cloud-native primitives.
